Validation

The long-chase bar

Tokens reach the bar only on claim, never on completion — so the bar is gated by claim behaviour, not just by play. A chest becomes claimable when the bar crosses its threshold; claiming it is optional and does not gate progression. Crossing the final threshold strips token rewards from every remaining mission and covers the uncompleted ones.

Completion % is the share of served missions the player finishes — the Inventory tab sizes targets so this lands where you want it. Late join is calendric first-exposure proration: set days remaining and the chest effort and rewards are scaled by the PRD formulas.

Make the hands a mission needs

Ask for the events and this builds the 52-card deck that delivers them. Every hand is chosen against a live simulation of the round before it, so hits and dealer draws are accounted for rather than hoped for. Feasibility is checked first — a deck holds four aces, so some asks are arithmetically out of reach and the tool says so instead of searching forever.

Targets

Day plans — the decks a whole chain needs

A single deck is a single match, and a day's chain routinely asks for more events than one deck can hold — a target of 3 on open_blackjack fits in one deck, a target of 29 on open_same_color does not. So the deliverable for a day is an ordered sequence of decks. Each is built against whatever is still outstanding, and because missions progress in parallel one deck usually advances several at once.

Whole decks count here — a mission is scored across the entire match, not a hand window. Targets come straight from the Builder, so edit the chain there and re-plan.

Config factory

Generates a grid of complete configs — missions, targets, tokens, chests and prizes — and runs every one through a player-level simulation on the measured deck rates. The two axes are the two levers that actually trade off: how hard the targets are, and how much the run is designed to pay. Everything else is held fixed so the comparison stays readable.
